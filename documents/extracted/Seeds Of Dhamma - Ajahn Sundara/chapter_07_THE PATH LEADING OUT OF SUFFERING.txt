## THE PATH LEADING OUT OF SUFFERING

We start on the path of practice in a state of not-knowing (avijjā) – passion, greed, selfishness, discontent and laziness are very much part of the make-up of our mind/body experience. When the clear mirror of mindfulness is not well developed, there is a natural tendency to identify with what goes on in the mind.

However through the practice of meditation, looking directly into our various states of mind, we notice that these mind-states arise and pass away. We understand that all we experience is a continuous flow – a flow of thoughts, feelings, emotions, perceptions, memories – empty of any solid or permanent entity.

As we begin to see that everything is inherently anicca (impermanent), dukkha (painful) and anattā (not-self), we begin to understand the suffering of identification with mind and body. We also begin to understand the path leading out of that suffering. This is Dhamma.
