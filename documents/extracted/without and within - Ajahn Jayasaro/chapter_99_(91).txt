There appears to be a significant amount of crime and corruption in Thailand. How is this possible in such a thoroughly Buddhist country? (91)

Crime and corruption are universal. Religious affiliation or a lack of it is only one factor amongst many determining the level of crime in a society. Poverty, for one, is a more reliable indicator. Nevertheless, it might be supposed that if a relationship were to be established between the problems faced by a society and its dominant religion, that relationship would be found in:

i) people justifying harmful actions with religious teachings

ii) people justifying harmful actions by distorting religious teachings

iii) people acting in direct opposition to the teachings of their religion

Amongst Thais who consider themselves Buddhist, i) is unknown, ii) is rare and iii) is common.

It could also be argued that the degree of crime and corruption in Thailand is an indication of how little an attempt has been made by its political leaders to ensure that Buddhist values are upheld in a period of rapid social and economic change.
