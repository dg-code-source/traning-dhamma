## Glossary

anattā	literally, ‘not-self’ i.e., impersonal, without individual 	essence; neither a person nor belonging to a person; one of 	the three characteristics of conditioned phenomena arahant	a fully enlightened person; according to the Pali Canon, the fourth stage on the path avijjā	ignorance bhava-taṇhā	desire to become, achieve or obtain something Buddha-rupa	an image of the Buddha brahmacariya	holy/spiritual life (specifically monastic) brahmavihāra	sublime states: loving-kindness, compassion, sympathetic joy and equanimity citta	mind, heart, psyche desanā	a talk on the teachings of the Buddha Dhamma	the way it is, the true order of reality, the Buddha’s teachings dhutaṅga 	(Thai ‘tudong’) special renunciant observances, austere practices dukkha	suffering, stress, unsatisfactoriness; one of the three characteristics of conditioned phenomena farang	(Thai) foreigner jhāna	state of absorption kāma-taṇhā	sense desire, desire for sensual pleasure karuṇā	compassion kilesa	defilement, unwholesome qualities that cloud the mind kor wat	(Thai) The list of duties to be observed with reference to the dwellings, requisites and one’s fellow samaṇas. Used with great care, it is one of the principle means of training in Ajahn Chah’s monasteries. kuti	a secluded and simple dwelling for a monk or nun mettā	loving kindness, goodwill muditā	sympathetic joy nibbāna 	(equivalent to Sanskrit nirvana) literally ‘extinguishing of a fire’; freedom from attachments, quenching, coolness paññā	discernment, wisdom pariyatti	theory, getting an intellectual grasp of a teaching; the academic aspects of Dhamma paṭipatti  	putting understanding into practice pativedha	realizing the meaning of the teachings in oneself phra farang	(Thai) foreign monk poo roo	(Thai) being the one who knows saddhā	confidence, faith sakkāya-diṭṭhi	personality-view, the first fetter that has to be broken for the realization of nibbāna, i.e. enlightenment samādhi	meditative concentration, unification of mind, collectedness samaṇa	contemplative, renunciant, religious seeker, monk or nun samanera	novice monk samatha	calm, tranquillity, steadying sampajañña	clear comprehension, full awareness sati	mindfulness sīla	morality, virtue, precept sotāpanna,sakadagami,anagami,arahant	 the four stages of enlightenment (stream-enterer, once-returner, non-returner, fully enlightened person) sotāpanna	literally ‘stream-enterer’, one whose realization has transcended the first three ‘fetters’ or mental structures that block awakening, i.e. identification with one’s personality; attachment to customs and systems; and wavering uncertainty as to Dhamma. Having transcended these, a ‘stream-enterer’ is said to inevitably realize complete awakening within a maximum of seven lifetimes tudong	(Thai, from Pali dhutaṅga) the practice of walking for weeks or months in remote places with no guarantees of food or lodgings upādāna	attachment, clinging, grasping upasampadā	admission to the Bhikkhu-Sangha upekkhā	equanimity vibhava-taṇhā	desire to get rid of, desire for oblivion or annihilation vicikicchā	sceptical doubt viññāṇa     	consciousness vipāka-kamma 	results of previous intentional action
